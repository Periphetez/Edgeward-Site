The gap we close

Modern small units carry drones and they carry ATAK. The gap nobody has cleanly filled is the software in between — the layer that turns operator intent on a tactical map into autonomous ISR missions, and turns drone footage into actionable intelligence the operator can trust.

Edgeward is that layer. Operators draw a zone, mark a point of interest, or request an orbit. Edgeward plans the mission, runs perception against the feed, and reports back through the same map.

Capabilities

One software layer, four jobs.

Perception

Real-time person and vehicle detection and tracking on edge hardware, with ground-target geolocation. Flight-validated on real hardware.

Avoidance

Detects and tracks nearby aircraft from a single monocular camera. Bearings-only tracking, adapted from published naval and aviation collision-avoidance research, estimates time-to-closest-approach without needing LiDAR or absolute range.

Coordination

Bidirectional Cursor-on-Target. Operators draw zones and points of interest on the map they already use; Edgeward ingests them as live behavior policy and publishes alerts and proposed missions back.

Execution

Planned: generates orbit, lawnmower, and patrol routes from operator intent, for review and approval before execution. Currently in development — not yet flight-validated.

Current status
  • Flight-validated: onboard person and vehicle detection, ground-target geolocation, and live two-way ATAK/CoT reporting — proven in real flight testing, September 2026.
  • Bench and simulation-validated: aircraft-to-aircraft detect-and-avoid from a single monocular camera. Bearings-only tracking validated to 0.8–5.0% time-to-closest-approach accuracy (static scenarios). Airborne validation is the next milestone.
  • In development: autonomous mission planning and execution.
