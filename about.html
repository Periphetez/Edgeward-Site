About

One place to task every autonomous vehicle, and see everything they find.

Edgeward was founded on a simple belief: autonomous vehicles are the future, and the teams using them will need one platform to control them all and one place where all their data comes together.

We didn't want to build yet another tool for operators to learn. Teams already plan, talk and track each other in TAK. So we asked a simple question: why not use the tool that already exists? Edgeward puts the tasking and the results from autonomous work there, so every aircraft, every plan and every find sits on one map.

The next step is autonomy that does more of the work. Today a drone needs a person at every step. We're building toward missions the aircraft can complete on their own: plan, fly, search, stay clear of other traffic and report back, with the operator approving decisions instead of flying. That is how a small team delivers mass at scale.

Edgeward is led by an RCAF veteran who has run long-range autonomous drone operations for wildfire and national security missions, and built autonomy and control systems for ground vehicles and heavy equipment on active work sites. We start from what the operator needs, use open standards so nobody is locked in, and say exactly how far each capability has been tested.

How we work

Three rules we build by.

01

Start with the operator

Software should fit the tools and habits a team already has, starting with the TAK devices they carry.

02

Open standards

MAVLink in, Cursor-on-Target out. Your aircraft and your data are never locked to us.

03

Honest about testing

Simulation, hardware-in-the-loop, then flight. We say which stage each capability has reached.
